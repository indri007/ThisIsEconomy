To reduce this thesis to a software artifact—to speak only of IndoBERT transformers, Louvain community partitions, and F1-score matrices—is to forget why science exists.

Food is not a fiscal stimulus package. A school meal is not an electoral token.

In every culture since the dawn of our species, feeding another person’s child is a sacred, inviolable trust. It is the unspoken vow that binds a society together: *“Whatever our differences, whatever our poverty, our children will not go hungry under our watch.”*

When that food is poisoned by neglect, when that food is degraded by greed, it is not merely a program that fails. **It is the social contract itself that rots from the inside out.**

This research did not exist to criticize; it existed to bear witness. To ensure that when the history of this program is written in gold-leaf government reports, the digital archive will forever preserve the unvarnished truth: the tears disguised as sarcasm, the quiet nausea of betrayed mothers, and the dignity of a people who, even in the depths of hunger, still knew the difference between a gift of love and a meal of ash.
